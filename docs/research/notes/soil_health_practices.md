# Soil health practices (not fertilizer) for rainfed farms in Central Anatolia (İç Anadolu)

Evidence tags: **[P]** = I read it on the page or in the full PDF text. **[S]** = search-engine snippet or summary only, not checked against the full text. Treat [S] numbers as `verified: false`.
Unit note: 1 da = 0.1 ha, so 100 kg/da = 1 t/ha.

## 1. Conservation, reduced and no-till (doğrudan ekim) in wheat–fallow and wheat–legume systems

### Takeaway
In Konya, no-till (DE) gives wheat yields about the same as conventional tillage (GE) on average. It can lose badly in single years when sowing date or weed control goes wrong. Only about 2% of Konya's wheat area was under no-till in a 2014 survey; the main barriers are fear of yield loss, weeds and having no local example to see.

### Cited Findings
- **Bahri Dağdaş (Konya), 2011–2014 trial.** Treatments: GE vs DE, with wheat after fallow, chickpea, lentil, Hungarian vetch or safflower. Konya rainfall averages 318 mm/yr and about 90% falls in the wheat season; the last trial year was about 30% drier. Mean wheat yield was 226 kg/da under GE and 241 kg/da under DE. By year the results flipped:
  - year 1: GE 234 vs DE 204 kg/da
  - year 2: GE 271 vs DE 218 kg/da
  - year 3 (dry): GE 174 vs DE 301 kg/da

  Year-to-year swings were put down to sowing date and weed density (DE vetch plots were overrun by *Hordeum marinum*). All plots got 7 kg/da P2O5 and 7 kg/da N in total. — [Gültekin et al. 2017, KSÜ Doğa Bil. Derg. 20 (Özel Sayı):283-287](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/390551) [P]
- **Same paper, regional context.** Wheat covers about 70% of field-crop area in Central Anatolia. Of about 5 million ha of field crops, about 2.5 million ha is left as tilled fallow each year. Fallow tillage can store up to 29% of the rain it receives. — [Gültekin et al. 2017](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/390551) [P] (the paper cites "Anonim 2017" for this)
- **Definition used in Turkey.** No-till (DE) means sowing into the previous crop's stubble with no tillage from harvest to sowing, opening less than 20% of the soil surface. FAO counts it as conservation agriculture only when combined with permanent cover and rotation. — [Gültekin et al. 2017](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/390551) [P]
- **Adoption in Konya (survey, March 2014; Kulu, Cihanbeyli, Altınekin, Sarayönü, Çumra).**
  - DE was used on 2% of the province's wheat area.
  - 43% of wheat farmers had never heard of DE; 57% knew of it.
  - Among DE users (Turkish abstract): 62% reported saving fuel and labour, 25% reported lower income from lower yield, and 13% reported heavy weeds. The English abstract gives 50% / 25% / 12.5%, so the paper contradicts itself.
  - Reasons for not adopting, among those who knew DE: yield loss (19.3%), seen on TV but never in a field (14.0%), too few others use it (12.3%), belief that soil needs several tillage passes (12.3%), fear of weeds and disease (10.5%).
  - Demonstrations were held on the poorest land. In one village where 300 kg/da is normal, a bad plot choice gave 100 kg/da, and neighbours read this as "DE cuts yield". — [Küçükçongar, Kan, Özdemir 2014, Bahri Dağdaş Bitkisel Araştırma Dergisi (1-2):26-35](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/253678) [P]
- **Review of Turkish studies.** Plough tillage destroys aggregates and causes organic-matter loss. A single ploughing can cost up to 11% of soil organic matter (international citation, Conant et al.). One Turkish trial found no significant yield difference between tillage systems, with less fuel used (the exact l/da figure was garbled in extraction). Another found DE gave yields similar to conventional in Central Anatolia (Çarman et al. 2013). The review recommends a legume–cereal system with minimum or no-till instead of black fallow–wheat/barley. — [Akkaya 2016, Tarla Bitkileri Merkez Araştırma Enst. Derg. 25(Özel-1):280-291](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/262911) [P]
- **Fallow efficiency depends on soil depth.** In a 2-year Central Anatolia study, fallow efficiency was about 18% on a 90 cm soil profile. Fallow was not effective at all on a 40 cm profile (Başkan & Ünver 2000). — [Akkaya 2016](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/262911) [P]
- **Long-term conservation agriculture (CA) experiment in a cold, arid Central Anatolia site (2025).** Compared CA with conventional tillage under 2- and 4-year rotations. The authors report superior wheat yield and profit under CA with diversified rotations. They say earlier Turkish no-till studies showed benefits only for wheat–fallow or wheat–wheat, and only in economic terms. — [Land 2025, 14(7):1331, doi:10.3390/land14071331](https://doi.org/10.3390/land14071331) [S]: the MDPI page returned 403, so site, effect sizes and soil organic carbon (SOC) data were not seen.
- **CIMMYT (2012 web repost of a 2007 E-News item).** Typical rainfed winter wheat on the Anatolian plateau yields about 2 t/ha. Zero-till demonstrations on a 1,700 ha farm three hours east of Ankara yielded more than 4 t/ha. A retired Turkish agronomist doubted zero-till for rainfed wheat: "I don't think you can retain enough moisture over the fallow period." — [CIMMYT](https://www.cimmyt.org/news/zero-tillage-a-winner-for-winter-wheat-in-turkey/) [P]. This is a demonstration, not a controlled trial.
- **FAO with CIMMYT/ICARDA.** There has been considerable no-till and CA research in Turkey, and Turkish firms build no-till seeders for export. — [FAO i3275e](https://www.fao.org/4/i3275e/i3275e.pdf) [S]
- **International support only, not regional.** Reduced or no-till raised SOC in the top 5 cm by 25–75% compared with ploughing. — the snippet did not give a source or study; [S], weak, do not use without the original paper.

### Inferences
- A farmer-facing message should be "no-till yields are about the same on average and save tillage passes; success depends on sowing on time and controlling weeds". It should not be "no-till raises yield".
- Fallow's moisture benefit only holds on deep soils, about 90 cm or more. On shallow soils (around 40 cm) tilled fallow stores almost nothing. That makes shallow soils the strongest case for cutting fallow or switching to no-till.

### Gaps
- No regional, measured soil organic carbon or soil-moisture effect of no-till was obtained from full text. The Land 2025 paper is the best lead but could not be fetched.
- No current (post-2014) adoption figure for Konya or national no-till area was found.

## 2. Stubble management and stubble burning (anız yakma)

### Takeaway
Stubble burning is banned under Environment Law 2872. The 2026 fine is about 698.62 TL per da, five times that next to forests, wetlands or settlements. Keeping stubble or straw mulch protects soil and moisture; old Turkish rainfed work reports large yield gains from straw mulch.

### Cited Findings
- **2026 fine.** Burning is prohibited under Çevre Kanunu 2872. The 2026 penalty is 698.62 TL per da, raised fivefold next to forests or wetlands or in settlements. Forest-adjacent cases also fall under the Forest Law (Orman Kanunu). — [TRT Haber, Bolu Valiliği notice, 2026](https://www.trthaber.com/haber/turkiye/boluda-aniz-yakanlara-dekar-basina-698-lira-ceza-uygulanacak-948830.html) [P]
- **Legal instruments.** The amount is set by the 2026/1 communiqué (Tebliğ) under Article 20 of Law 2872 (Resmî Gazete, 30.12.2025). An article 20(1)(k) communiqué, 2025/41, is also cited. — [alomaliye 2026/1](https://www.alomaliye.com/2025/12/30/2026-yili-cevre-kanunu-uyarinca-verilecek-idari-para-cezalari/); [FAOLEX/LEAP 2025/41](https://leap.unep.org/en/countries/tr/national-legislation/notification-no-202541-administrative-fines-according-paragraph-k) [S]
- **Criminal exposure.** If a stubble fire spreads into forest, the Forest Law allows prison terms of 1 to 10 years. — [search summary over news sources](https://www.timeturk.com/aniz-yakan-ureticiye-agir-fatura-ceza-5-katina-cikabilir) [S]
- **Effect of keeping stubble.** Keeping stubble raises soil quality, organic matter and moisture retention and prevents soil loss (Turmel et al.). When tillage, burning and erosion act together, the organic-matter loss is much larger. In a Canadian study, the tillage effect was larger than the burning effect. — [Akkaya 2016](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/262911) [P]
- **Straw mulch rates.** Under Turkish rainfed conditions, 1000 kg/da of straw gave a +40% yield (Gerek 1968, Eskişehir; old study). International support: 675 kg/da straw mulch raised winter wheat water-use efficiency by 17% and yield by 16% (Huang et al.). No-till without residue could not repair soil physical properties (Zhang et al.). — [Akkaya 2016](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/262911) [P]

### Inferences
- The cheapest soil-health action is "do not burn, leave the stubble". It is legally required and costs nothing.
- A mulch of 1000 kg/da is well above typical straw output from about 200–300 kg/da of grain. Stubble is also often baled or grazed. Realistic retention will be partial.
- The fines are in TL. Under the project rule (no money shown on the site), present this as "illegal; administrative fine applies" without amounts.

### Gaps
- I did not see the official Resmî Gazete text myself; the amount comes from news reports.
- Whether stubble burning cuts eligibility for farm support (destek kesintisi) was not confirmed.

## 3. Soil organic matter: levels and how to raise it

### Takeaway
Most Konya soils are low in organic matter: about 70% count as poor and about 90% are below 3%. The soils are rich in lime and potassium and poor in phosphorus. I found no regional figure for realistic annual organic-matter gains.

### Cited Findings
- **Konya, 17,521 bulked soil samples.** 24.5% had <1% organic matter, 45.3% had 1–2%, and 20.5% had 2–3%. About 70% of Konya soils are poor in organic matter and 90% are below 3%. This matches a national study 40 years earlier: of 30,046 samples, 21% had <1%, 54.6% had 1–2% and 18.3% had 2–3%. — [Akkaya 2016](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/262911), citing Gezgin et al. and Ülgen & Yurtsever 1974 [P]
- **Karapınar soils.** "Rich in lime and potassium, very poor in organic matter and phosphorus." — [Konya Toprak Su ve Çölleşme ile Mücadele Araştırma Enstitüsü, history page](https://arastirma.tarimorman.gov.tr/konyatopraksu/Menu/13/Tarihce) [P]
- **Karatay–Çengilti local study.** Lime 45.9–71.7%, organic matter 0.27–2.47%. In another Konya study, 75% of samples were low in organic matter, with clay texture and high lime. — [DergiPark search results](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/3115681) [S]
- **Organic matter fractions.** 10–40% of soil organic matter is active and 40–60% is stable humus. Stable organic matter has about 5 times the cation-exchange capacity of clay. In these soils low organic matter also limits water holding. — [Akkaya 2016](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/262911) [P]
- **Lentil vs fallow, Central Anatolia (Adak et al. 1998).** Shallow tillage improved microbial activity in the root zone. Lentil–wheat plots were slightly better in organic matter than fallow–wheat, and lentil improved organic matter and N deeper in the profile. — [Akkaya 2016](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/262911) [P]

### Inferences
- In 300 mm rainfed systems, the main levers are to stop losses (no burning, less ploughing, fewer fallow-tillage passes, keep residue) and add legumes. Manure and compost are local options where livestock is present.

### Gaps
- No regional trial gave a measured organic-matter gain per year (e.g., % per year or t C/ha per year) from manure, compost, residue or green manure. Do not invent one; any site number would have to be an "assumption".

## 4. Legume rotations replacing fallow (chickpea, lentil, vetch/fiğ)

### Takeaway
In Konya's 318 mm conditions, fallow still gave the highest following-wheat yield (286 kg/da). Wheat after lentil (249) was not significantly lower in one year. Winter legumes (winter lentil, Hungarian vetch, winter vetch) suit wheat better than spring chickpea or lentil, which dry the profile. Nitrogen left for the next wheat is only a few kg N/da.

### Cited Findings
- **Konya 2011–2014, 3-year mean wheat yield by previous crop.** Fallow 286 kg/da, lentil 249, chickpea 228, Hungarian vetch 223, safflower 182. As a share of after-fallow yield: lentil about 87%, chickpea about 80%, vetch about 78%, safflower about 64%. In year 2, the fallow–lentil difference was not significant, and the highest single treatment was DE after lentil. — [Gültekin et al. 2017](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/390551) [P]
- **Ankara PhD study.** Spring lentil and chickpea left a lot of inorganic N in the profile but used more water. The dry soil at harvest also tilled badly, so the following wheat yielded less. Wheat after winter legumes (Hungarian vetch, winter lentil) yielded more. — [YÖK Açık Bilim: "Orta Anadolu... ikili ekim nöbeti... nem ve inorganik azot"](https://acikbilim.yok.gov.tr/handle/20.500.12812/514487) [S]
- **Ankara trial.** Winter vetch–wheat is recommended over fallow–wheat for higher grain yield. Winter lentil is a better previous crop than spring lentil for soil moisture. — [Adak et al., Turk J Agric For 25(4)](https://aj.tubitak.gov.tr/agriculture/issues/tar-01-25-4/tar-25-4-6-0006-54.pdf) [S] (host failed DNS when fetched)
- **Haymana (Ankara).** Wheat after chickpea, lentil or vetch yielded 308.9–354.8 kg/da, against 346.0 kg/da after fallow. — search summary of [Turk J Agric For 25(4)](https://aj.tubitak.gov.tr/agriculture/issues/tar-01-25-4/tar-25-4-6-0006-54.pdf) or neighbouring paper [S]
- **N to the next wheat (TAEK 15N study, Central Anatolia rainfed).** Residual N carried to wheat was 43.0 kg N/ha (about 4.3 kg N/da) after winter lentil and 17.0 kg N/ha (about 1.7 kg N/da) after spring lentil. — [TENMAK archive](https://kurumsalarsiv.tenmak.gov.tr/server/api/core/bitstreams/ba3db534-ee00-4791-a429-5df548f1e533/content) [S]
- **Annual N fixation of grain legumes (textbook values).** Generally 5–19 kg N/da per year: faba bean 19, lentil 12, pea 9, chickpea 8, bean 5. — [Ankara Üniv. açık ders](https://acikders.ankara.edu.tr/mod/resource/view.php?id=54309) [S]. Fixation is not the same as the credit to the next crop; much leaves with the grain.

### Inferences
- The site should show "legume instead of fallow" as a trade-off for the next wheat: about 13–22% lower in the Konya trial, and the gap shrinks in some years. Gains are a crop in the fallow year, plus N and organic matter.
- This fits the repo guardrail that next wheat should not fall below 85% of after-fallow. In the Konya data, lentil (about 87%) passes. Chickpea (about 80%) and vetch (about 78%) fail on 3-year means, but vetch performed better in other trials (Ankara, Haymana).
- Prefer winter-sown legumes.

### Gaps
- No full-text check of the 15N numbers or the Haymana yields.
- No regional data on legume effects on soil structure (aggregates, infiltration).

## 5. Cover crops and green manure in 250–400 mm dryland

### Takeaway
I found no regional evidence that a true non-harvested cover crop works within a wheat–fallow system at 250–400 mm. The closest evidence is green manure replacing fallow, which improved some durum wheat yield components (2003–05). The practical form is a legume in the fallow year, sown in winter and harvested or cut for hay early.

### Cited Findings
- **Green manure vs fallow.** A rainfed trial (2003–2005) tested green-manure crops in place of fallow for durum wheat. Green manure increased some yield components compared with fallow. — [Karakurt et al., DergiPark 262795](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/262795) [S]
- **Green manure against fallow erosion.** Long-term fallow–wheat can increase erosion; growing at least a green manure during fallow is suggested to reduce it. — [Turk J Agric For 25(4)](https://aj.tubitak.gov.tr/agriculture/issues/tar-01-25-4/tar-25-4-6-0006-54.pdf) [S]
- **Vetch cut early for hay.** Hungarian vetch sown with triticale was cut green in early June in the Konya trial. Without weed control, the weedy grass *Hordeum marinum* spread into the following wheat. — [Gültekin et al. 2017](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/390551) [P]

### Inferences
- With about 90% of rain falling in the wheat season (Konya), a cover crop competes directly with fallow water storage. Advise it only as "legume or forage in the fallow year, terminated early". It carries a risk to next wheat that should go through the guardrail.

### Gaps
- No regional measured soil-water cost of a cover or green manure, and no site location or yield numbers from the Karakurt study.

## 6. Wind erosion (Karapınar) and water erosion

### Takeaway
Karapınar is Turkey's classic wind-erosion case: migration was threatened in the 1960s, and control started in 1962. The methods are reed screens, grassing, tree belts, fencing out grazing, and fallow–grain strips set across the wind. These are directly reusable as farm-level advice.

### Cited Findings
- **History.** Severe wind erosion threatened Karapınar with migration in the 1960s. Control began in 1962 under TOPRAKSU, starting with wire-fencing 160,000 da. — [Konya Toprak Su ve Çölleşme ile Mücadele Araştırma Enstitüsü, Tarihçe](https://arastirma.tarimorman.gov.tr/konyatopraksu/Menu/13/Tarihce) [P]
- **Areas.**
  - Dune field: 43,000 da.
  - Mobile dunes fenced and grassed: 40,000 da.
  - Flat erosion-prone land: 32,000 da (10,000 restored for farming, 20,000 under strip cropping, 2,000 demonstration orchards).
  - Ketir Tepesi basalt area: 15,000 da, with about 700,000 almond trees planted.
  - Karapınar's farmland totals 1,595,000 da, of which 1,030,000 da is affected by wind erosion. About 450,000 da is now irrigated. — [same page](https://arastirma.tarimorman.gov.tr/konyatopraksu/Menu/13/Tarihce) [P]
- **Methods.**
  - Reed screens 1.5–2.0 m tall, set perpendicular to the prevailing wind, 12–20 m apart (8–10 times their height), with stakes every 2 m.
  - Grass sown between the screens: sand rye, *Agropyron cristatum*, *A. elongatum*.
  - Permanent tree belts: oleaster (iğde), acacia, ash, elm, maple.
  - Fallow–grain strips 40–60 m wide across the wind.
  - Deep ploughing with plough and disc harrow is named as a cause of erosion. — [same page](https://arastirma.tarimorman.gov.tr/konyatopraksu/Menu/13/Tarihce) [P]
- **Cost of the early programme.** The 1962–72 programme cost about 14.8 million TL at 1972 prices. — [YÖK thesis, Karapınar rüzgar erozyonu](https://acikbilim.yok.gov.tr/handle/20.500.12812/425328) [S]
- **Ongoing research.** A 2022–2024 project tests shrub-form plants as windbreaks on arid and semi-arid rangeland, Karapınar included. — [institute project list](https://arastirma.tarim.gov.tr/konyatopraksu/Belgeler/Tarımsal-Araştırma-Projeleri+.pdf) [S]
- **Groundwater irrigation and sinkholes.** The DESIRE (EU) policy brief on Karapınar covers groundwater-based irrigation, which followed erosion control, and sinkhole and land-degradation risks. — [DESIRE policy brief](https://desire-his.wur.nl/index.php/en/download-documents/doc_view/Policy%20brief%20Karapinar_TR.pdf_%3b%20modification-date%3d_Tue%2c%2031%20Jan%202012%2010_53_41%20%2b0100_%3b%20size%3d2390552%3b) [S, not opened]

### Inferences
- Farm-level wind-erosion advice:
  - keep stubble standing over the dry, windy season;
  - set strips and tillage across the prevailing wind;
  - avoid fine seedbeds from repeated disc passes;
  - plant hedgerows or tree belts on field edges.

### Gaps
- No regional numbers for erosion rates (t/ha/yr) on Central Anatolian rainfed fields. No regional contour-tillage trial was found; water-erosion advice would have to be generic.

## 7. Salinity, lime/alkalinity, compaction

### Takeaway
Salinity and sodicity in the Konya closed basin are mainly a problem on irrigated, poorly drained lowland (e.g., Çumra), not on typical rainfed upland wheat fields. The fix is drainage, leaching and gypsum on sodic soils. The soils are highly calcareous, which lime cannot "fix"; it is managed (phosphorus and micronutrient availability). No regional compaction or controlled-traffic data was found.

### Cited Findings
- **Basin scale.** Salinity and sodicity affect 509,382 ha of the basin, about 12% of 4.33 million ha. The figure is based on an old (1978) inventory. — [DergiPark 3115681](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/3115681) [S]
- **Çumra.** About 59,000 ha irrigated, with drainage problems and a fast-rising water table after rain. Gypsum is the main amendment for sodic soils. — [DergiPark search results](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/3536892) [S]
- **Karapınar.** One Karapınar soil study found no salinity. — [search summary](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/252104) [S]
- **Lime.** Karatay soils hold 45.9–71.7% lime. Karapınar soils are rich in lime and poor in phosphorus. — [DergiPark 3115681](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/3115681) [S]; [Institute history page](https://arastirma.tarimorman.gov.tr/konyatopraksu/Menu/13/Tarihce) [P]
- **Compaction.** Heavy machinery and working soil outside proper moisture (tav) damage soil structure and raise bulk density. — [Akkaya 2016](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/262911) [P] (qualitative only)

### Inferences
- For a rainfed site, flag salinity only for low-lying or irrigated parcels, or where a salt crust or white surface is seen. Recommend a soil test (EC, pH, exchangeable sodium percentage (ESP)) before using gypsum.

### Gaps
- No regional gypsum rate or response trial, and no compaction or controlled-traffic study for Central Anatolia.

## 8. Cheap soil-health indicators a farmer can check

### Takeaway
I found no Turkish-regional source for a farmer-level soil-health kit. Practical proxies grounded in the sources above are an organic-matter test in a standard lab analysis, crusting and infiltration after rain, and soil depth (which decides whether fallow pays).

### Cited Findings
- **The top 10 cm controls crop establishment.** It drives germination, crusting, aeration, infiltration, erosion and runoff. — [Akkaya 2016](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/262911) [P]
- **Active organic matter is a better fertility indicator than total organic matter.** — [Akkaya 2016](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/262911) [P]
- **Profile depth decides fallow efficiency.** About 18% at 90 cm; not effective at 40 cm. — [Akkaya 2016](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/262911) [P]

### Inferences
- Candidate site checklist:
  - organic-matter % from a soil test, flagged when below 1–2%;
  - soil depth, with a spade or auger to 40/90 cm;
  - surface crust after rain;
  - stubble cover on the surface after sowing;
  - earthworms and slake/aggregate tests, from generic international methods only.

### Gaps
- No regional thresholds or protocols (e.g., Turkish extension guides on infiltration or slake tests, or earthworm counts) were found.

## 9. Turkish policy and support

### Takeaway
The legal frame is Law 5403 (soil conservation, erosion-sensitive areas, binding land-use plans) plus the 2872 burning ban. ÇATAK (Çevre Amaçlı Tarım Arazilerini Koruma) is the agri-environment payment, with Category I/II/III rates of 45/60/135 TL/da on the ministry page (undated; appear to be from 2015). I could not confirm current status, current amounts, or no-till machinery grants.

### Cited Findings
- **Law 5403 (Toprak Koruma ve Arazi Kullanımı Kanunu, 2005).** Covers soil and land inventory, land-use planning, and agricultural land-use plans and projects. Erosion-sensitive areas can be designated, and plans are prepared by governorships. A new implementing regulation (April 2026) makes compliance with plans mandatory, with action under Article 20 of 5403. — [Resmî Gazete 2005 implementing regulation](https://www.resmigazete.gov.tr/eskiler/2005/12/20051215-5.htm); [alomaliye, 04.04.2026](https://www.alomaliye.com/2026/04/04/arazi-kullanim-planlamasi-uygulama-yonetmeligi/) [S]
- **ÇATAK page.**
  - Payment categories I: 45, II: 60, III: 135 TL/da, with no year or definitions.
  - The process is a pre-application to the provincial or district directorate, then a provincial commission, a commitment letter, a grant agreement and a field check; years 2–3 are checked against the crop declaration (ÇKS).
  - The only dated schedule shown is for 2015.
  - The page does not list supported practices. — [Tarım ve Orman Bakanlığı, ÇATAK Desteği](https://www.tarimorman.gov.tr/Konular/Tarimsal-Destekler/Diger-Tarimsal-Amacli-Destekler/Catak-Destegi) [P]
- **ÇATAK aims and funding.** The programme aims to protect soil and water and reduce erosion. It started in 2006 with external funding and has been domestically funded since the second half of 2009. — [ministry search snippet](https://www.tarimorman.gov.tr/Konular/Bitkisel-Uretim/Tarla-Ve-Bahce-Bitkileri/CATAK) [S]
- **Policy recommendations from Konya researchers.** Develop and distribute seeders suited to no-till and reduced tillage in Konya, and hold demonstrations on representative land rather than the worst land. — [Akkaya 2016](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/262911) [P]; [Küçükçongar et al. 2014](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/253678) [P]

### Inferences
- The site can say "programmes exist (ÇATAK; erosion and land-use rules under 5403); ask your district agriculture directorate". It should show no TL amounts, per the project's no-money rule.

### Gaps
