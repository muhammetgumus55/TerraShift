## 1. Fertilizer consumption in Turkey and Central Anatolia by product and nutrient (2019–2025)

### Takeaway
In 2023–2025 Turkey used about 6.5–7.0 million t of fertilizer product per year, or 2.6–2.8 million t of nutrients. Nitrogen makes up about two thirds of that. Urea is the largest product (28–30%), followed by CAN 26%, ammonium sulfate, 20-20-0 (with and without Zn) and DAP. The 13 Central Anatolian provinces take 28–30% of national N+P2O5+K2O, and Konya alone takes about 10–11% of national N. Use fell sharply in 2021–2022 (DAP −54% in 2021 vs 2020) and recovered in 2023–2024.

### Inferences
- The province figures are probably sales or distribution records by province (Gübre Takip Sistemi), not field application by province. Kırıkkale's N jumped from 15,104 t (2020) to 57,943 t (2023) and fell back to 21,680 t (2025), which looks like distributor relocation rather than agronomy. The metadata file "Gübre İstatistikleri Metaverisi.docx" on the BÜGEM page should be checked before using province numbers as on-farm use.
- The regional product mix is N-heavy: base DAP or 20-20-0, then urea, CAN or AS top dressing, and almost no K. This fits wheat and barley on calcareous, K-rich soils.
- The ratios suggest that Zn-containing 20-20-0 has been taking share from plain 20-20-0. In 2024 the Zn version was 41% of all 20-20-0-type product, up from 30% in 2019 [derived].

### Gaps

- I found no province-level kg/da series (nutrient tonnes ÷ cultivated area). It needs TÜİK area by province, which I did not fetch. The national FAO kg/ha series in SBB-ÖİK stops at 2020.
- I did not reach TOGÜSAD, TEPGE gübre or IFASTAT pages. No TEPGE fertilizer "durum-tahmin" report appears in the TEPGE list ([TEPGE list](https://arastirma.tarimorman.gov.tr/tepge/Menu/36/Durum-Ve-Tahmin-Raporlari), [snippet]).

## 2. Doses, timing and products used by wheat, barley and chickpea farmers (survey studies)

### Takeaway
Central Anatolian wheat farmers typically apply about 18–23 kg/da of DAP or 20-20-0 at sowing, then about 11–18 kg/da of urea and/or CAN as spring top dressing. The most recent Konya survey (Cihanbeyli, 2023 data) found 21 kg/da DAP and 11 kg/da urea. Most farmers decide doses from experience, and 60% said they had increased fertilizer use over the last 5 years. I found no surveys for barley or chickpea in the region.

### Cited Findings
- **Konya-Cihanbeyli, 2023 survey, published 2025** (Tapkı & Göz, *Wheat Studies* 14(1):1–10). Face-to-face survey of 70 wheat farms in 2023: 36 irrigated, 34 dry. Mean wheat yield was 392.57 kg/da. Both base and top fertilizer are used [seen-page]. [Tapkı & Göz 2025](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/4639595)

  | Input | Farms using it | Mean (range) |
  |---|---|---|
  | DAP 18-46 (base) | 60 | 21.00 kg/da (10–30) |
  | 20-20-0 (base) | 20 | 11.05 kg/da (9–12) |
  | Urea (top) | 67 | 11.12 kg/da (10–12) |
  | 26% nitrate / CAN (top) | 25 | 7.25 kg/da (6–8) |
  | Foliar fertilizer | 35 | 389 cc/da |

  - 60% of farmers said their fertilizer use rose over the last 5 years, 12.9% said it fell and 27.1% said it was unchanged.
  - Farmers said cutting inputs costs yield, while raising inputs raises costs a lot.
- Studies cited inside Tapkı & Göz 2025, as they report them (I did not check the originals) [seen-page]. [Tapkı & Göz 2025](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/4639595)
  - Öztürk et al. (2023, Muş, East Anatolia, for comparison only): 96% of farmers used DAP as base fertilizer; base averaged 18.7 kg/da and top 17.4 kg/da.
  - Kara et al. (2008): base 18.8 kg/da, top 17.7 kg/da.
  - Çelik & Sarıoğlu (2023, region not stated in the excerpt): DAP 22.89 kg/da, 20-20-0 29.89 kg/da, urea 43.49 kg/da.
- Ankara study (year not seen): mean of 19.64 kg DAP and 11.57 kg urea [snippet]. Possibly the Gölbaşı study below; not confirmed. [search result: silo.tips Gölbaşı PDF](https://silo.tips/download/buday-retmnde-toprak-analz-yaptirmanin-letme-zerne-etkler-ankara-glbai-les-rne-s)
- Konya study (probably "Konya İlinde Buğday Tarımının Genel Durumu ve Karşılaşılan Problemler", ResearchGate 339130821, year not seen) [snippet]. [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/339130821_Konya_Ilinde_Bugday_Tariminin_Genel_Durumu_ve_Karsilasilan_Problemler)
  - On dry land, most farmers who use base fertilizer use DAP (37% of farmers).
  - On irrigated land, urea and AN mixes are also used.
  - 19% apply no base fertilizer at all, 17% apply 11–15 kg/da DAP and 10% apply 16–20 kg/da.
- Equipment: no study gave fertilizer application equipment (centrifugal spreader vs combined seed drill). The Cihanbeyli paper gives tillage passes (2.28) and machine hours only [seen-page]. [Tapkı & Göz 2025](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/4639595)

### Inferences
- At 21 kg/da DAP plus 11 kg/da urea, Cihanbeyli farmers supply about 8.9 kg N/da and 9.7 kg P2O5/da [derived from product grades]. In a dry 300–400 kg/da wheat system that is a P-heavy ratio, in line with the province-level N:P2O5 of about 2:1. A site recommending lower P on P-sufficient soils would therefore differ from common practice.
- Dose ranges are narrow (urea SD 0.72 kg/da). That points to fixed habitual rates ("one bag per x dekar") rather than per-field calculation. This is an inference, not stated by the authors.

### Gaps
- I found no 2018–2026 survey with quantitative doses for barley or chickpea in Central Anatolia.
- I found no survey that compares farmer doses directly with official TAGEM/Topraksu recommendations for the same fields. The official recommendation tables exist ([TAGEM Topraksu gübre tavsiye verileri](https://www.tarimorman.gov.tr:443/TAGEM/Belgeler/yayin/TOPRAKSU%20GÜBRE%20TAVSİYE%20%20Verileri.pdf), [snippet], not opened).
- No source covered top-dressing timing in practice (tillering vs stem elongation) or split applications for the region.

## 3. Soil testing: share of farmers, reasons, and support policy history

### Takeaway
Soil testing in Central Anatolia was driven mainly by the support payments. From 2009 to May 2016, fertilizer support for parcels of 50 da or more required a soil analysis. In Konya, 43–90% of farms did analyses during that period, but only about half or fewer followed the recommendations; one Konya study found 96.1% did not. After 2016 the requirement was dropped. The 2025–2027 "temel destek" model does not appear to require soil analysis, though a separate soil-analysis support item is listed.

### Cited Findings
- Support history:
  - In 2009, to promote correct fertilizer use and soil analysis, eligibility for chemical fertilizer support on single parcels of 50 da or more was made conditional on a soil analysis of those parcels [seen-file]. [SBB-ÖİK 2025](https://www.sbb.gov.tr/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Gubre-Tohum-ve-Ilacta-Yerlilesme-OIK-Raporu_01082025.pdf)
  - The 8 May 2009 tebliğ combined mazot, chemical fertilizer and soil analysis support, requiring one analysis per 50 da [snippet]. [Alomaliye 2009](https://www.alomaliye.com/2009/05/08/ciftci-kayit-sistemine-dahil-olan-ciftcilere-mazot-kimyevi-gubre-ve-toprak-analizi-destekleme-odemesi-yapilmasina-dair-teblig-teblig-no-200941/)
  - A soil-analysis support for farmers began with a 2006 decision. From 2009 it was part of area-based support and continued until May 2016. The 5 May 2016 decision kept mazot and fertilizer support but made no mention of soil-analysis support [seen-page]. [Çönoğlu et al. 2016, Ege Üniv. Ziraat Fak. Derg. 53(4)](https://zfdergi.ege.edu.tr/edergiziraat/2016_cilt53/s4/10.pdf)
  - 2025–2027 model: mazot and fertilizer support became "temel destek", per decare for all ÇKS-registered producers. Search summaries do not show soil analysis as a precondition, but the Ministry lists a separate "Toprak Analizi Desteği" among its support items [snippet]. [Tarım ve Orman: Fark Ödemesi Destekleri](https://www.tarimorman.gov.tr/Konular/Tarimsal-Destekler/Fark-Odemesi-Destekleri); [Ministry news on the new model](https://www.tarimorman.gov.tr/Haber/6375/Bitkisel-Uretimde-Yeni-Destekleme-Modeli-Uygulamada)
- **Konya-Çumra** (Tanrıverdi & Çelik 2016, *Bahri Dağdaş Bitkisel Araştırma Dergisi* 5(1):35–44; 42 farms, stratified random sample, during the support era) [seen-page]. [Tanrıverdi & Çelik 2016](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/476123)
  - 90.48% of farms had soil analysis done.
  - 85.71% of those did it every year after the support started.
  - 76.93% had not done analysis before the support existed.
  - Reasons: to obtain Ministry support 58.30%, to raise yield 39.60%, the rest for "conscious farming".
  - 52.63% fertilized according to the analysis recommendations; 47.37% did not.
  - 54.76% thought result-based fertilization affected yield; 45.24% thought it was not effective enough.
  - The authors cite another study on why farmers who tested did not follow results: 34% did not trust the results and 18.8% gave a further reason that is cut off in the text I saw.
- **Konya** (Küçükçongar et al. 2014, as cited by Gözükara et al. 2022): 42.97% of farms did soil analysis, and 96.1% of those did not apply the fertilizers and amounts recommended [seen-page, secondary citation]. [Gözükara, Kutlu & Gülmezoğlu 2022, IJAWS 8(1):119–132](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/1924469)
- **Konya-Çumra, another stratified study** (authors and year not seen): 64.63% did soil analysis. Of these, 66.04% did it to get support and 32.08% for rational production, and 69.81% did not fully apply the results [snippet]. Candidate source: [dergipark 831671](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/831671).
- **Konya-Cihanbeyli** (Güldal & Özçelik, about 2017, Ankara Univ. thesis/article) [snippet]. [dergipark 321927](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/321927); [Ankara Univ. thesis record](https://avesis.ankara.edu.tr/yonetilen-tez/c42eeacb-f3eb-4e85-914d-af526f8853f2/bugday-yetistiriciliginde-toprak-analizi-sonucuna-gore-kullanilan-gubrenin-maliyete-etkilerinin-belirlenmesi-konya-ili-cihanbeyli-ilcesi-ornegi)
  - Among farms that did analysis, only 33.3% used fertilizer according to the results.
  - Among farms that did not, 71.4% fertilized by experience.
  - A chi-square test found no relationship between doing analysis and how the dose was set.
- **Ankara-Gölbaşı** (Küçükkaya, survey of 150 wheat growers in 2011): 17.64% found the analysis-recommended amount insufficient compared with their experience. Cost was among the reasons for not following results [snippet]. [silo.tips PDF](https://silo.tips/download/buday-retmnde-toprak-analz-yaptirmanin-letme-zerne-etkler-ankara-glbai-les-rne-s)
- **Comparison outside Central Anatolia** (İzmir, 2015 season, 118 farmers) [seen-page]. [Çönoğlu et al. 2016](https://zfdergi.ege.edu.tr/edergiziraat/2016_cilt53/s4/10.pdf)
  - 49.15% had soil analysis done.
  - 73.73% said they knew its benefits; 66.10% believed it is useful.
  - Motives on a 5-point scale: correct fertilizer and higher yield 4.50, knowing and protecting soil 4.02, using less fertilizer 3.10, getting support 2.38.
- **Eastern Anatolia, for contrast** (TRA1 region): 80.9% set fertilizer amounts by own experience, and only 4.7% decided after a soil analysis [snippet; exact paper not identified among the results].
- Labs: the Ministry authorizes soil and fertilizer analysis laboratories, and the BÜGEM page publishes an authorized-lab list (the "2024 Gübre Analizinde Yetkilendirilen Laboratuvarlar…" folder name) [seen-page, folder name only]. [BÜGEM statistics page](https://www.tarimorman.gov.tr/Konular/Bitkisel-Uretim/Bitki-Besleme-ve-Tarimsal-Teknolojiler/Bitki-Besleme-Istatistikleri)

### Inferences
- When analysis was tied to support, testing rates rose to 43–90% in Konya studies, but following the results stayed low: 4–53% depending on the study. Testing became a paperwork step more than a decision input. Removing the requirement in 2016 most likely lowered testing rates. I found no post-2016 Central Anatolia survey that measures this.
- Main barriers to following results: distrust of the lab and recommendation, a belief that the recommended dose is too low, and cost. A decision-support site should show why it recommends a dose, not just the number.

### Gaps
- I found no 2018–2026 survey of soil-testing rates in any Central Anatolian province.
- The current (2025–2027) soil-analysis support rules and conditions were not verified from the Resmi Gazete text.
- I did not find the number of accredited soil labs by province in Central Anatolia.

## 4. Known agronomic problems: excess N, P build-up, Zn deficiency, product confusion, urea losses

### Takeaway
Zinc deficiency in Central Anatolia's calcareous soils is well documented (Cakmak 1996: more than 90% of 76 soils below 0.5 mg/kg DTPA-Zn). It explains the large market share of 20-20-0+Zn and 15-15-15+Zn. Local soil surveys find P and K often adequate or high while Zn and Mn are low. I found no regional quantitative evidence for excess N, urea volatilization losses or DAP/20-20-0 confusion.

### Cited Findings
- Cakmak et al. 1996 (*Plant and Soil* 180:165–172), 76 Central Anatolian soils: mean pH 7.9, highly alkaline, and more than 90% had DTPA-Zn below 0.5 mg/kg, the usual critical level for calcareous soils. Where DTPA-Zn was below 0.15 mg/kg, Zn application raised grain yield by more than 31%. Durum wheat is more sensitive than bread wheat [snippet]. [avesis record](https://avesis.cu.edu.tr/yayin/d69ddf4e-8c58-4592-b3d5-d71440d8b1d5/zinc-deficiency-as-a-critical-problem-in-wheat-production-in-central-anatolia); [Cakmak IFA 2005](https://www.fertilizer.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/2005_IFA_KL_cakmak.pdf)
- Cakmak (IFA): Central Anatolia covers 50% of Turkey's wheat area (4.5 Mha). Wheat generally responded to Zn when DTPA-Zn was below 0.4 mg/kg. Zn application raised grain Zn concentration 2–3 times. Soil plus foliar Zn is recommended [snippet]. [Cakmak IFA 2005](https://www.fertilizer.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/2005_IFA_KL_cakmak.pdf)
- Eskişehir-Seyitgazi wheat soils (Gözükara et al. 2022, 45 samples): total N and available P were adequate, exchangeable K, Ca and Mg were high, and available Mn and Zn were low in both great soil groups. B was low in Brown Forest soils [seen-page]. [Gözükara et al. 2022](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/1924469)
- The same paper names "no water and no fertilization by soil analysis" as main factors limiting yield, citing Boling et al. 2010 and Küçükçongar et al. 2014 [seen-page]. [Gözükara et al. 2022](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/1924469)
- Eskişehir and the western transition zone: Ministry station research says P recommendations can be based on soil-test values, but soil analysis is not enough for N decisions [snippet]. [GKTAEM: Eskişehir ve Batı Geçit Kuşağı buğday–azot](https://arastirma.tarimorman.gov.tr/gktaem/Belgeler/100.%20YIL/ESKİŞEHİR%20VE%20BATI%20GEÇİT%20KUŞAĞI%20BÖLGESİNDE%20BUĞDAY%20-%20AZOT%20İLİŞKİSİ%20ÜZERİNE%20ARAŞTIRMALAR.pdf)
- Zn products: national 20-20-0+Zn use was 472,858 t in 2024 and 450,136 t in 2025, and 15-15-15+Zn was 139,016 t in 2025. Zn-containing fertilizer has been sold nationally since 2000, when 20-20-0+Zn was 159,116 t [seen-file]. [BÜGEM-ürün](https://www.tarimorman.gov.tr/Konular/Bitkisel-Uretim/Bitki-Besleme-ve-Tarimsal-Teknolojiler/Bitki-Besleme-Istatistikleri)
- Nitrates policy: Turkey's Nitrates Regulation (2004, revised 23 July 2016) requires designating nitrate-vulnerable zones and good agricultural practice codes. The Green Deal Action Plan targets reduced chemical fertilizer use [seen-file]. [SBB-ÖİK 2025](https://www.sbb.gov.tr/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Gubre-Tohum-ve-Ilacta-Yerlilesme-OIK-Raporu_01082025.pdf)

### Inferences
- K2O use in Central Anatolian provinces is tiny (Section 1), and local soils test K-high. A site that recommends little or no K is therefore consistent with practice. Zn is where soil testing and practice most plausibly diverge: farmers may buy +Zn products by habit, or skip them, regardless of soil Zn.
- Gözükara et al. found P adequate even with long-term DAP and 20-20-0 use, which suggests P build-up in some Central Anatolian fields. This is not quantified at regional scale.

### Gaps
- I found no regional quantitative study of N surplus or N-use efficiency for Central Anatolian wheat.
- I found no source on surface-applied urea volatilization losses specifically in Central Anatolia.
- I found no source documenting farmers mixing up DAP and 20-20-0.

## 5. Farmer information sources, trust in recommendations, and extension gaps

### Takeaway
Experience, family and neighbors dominate fertilizer decisions. Some farmers distrust or under-rate soil-test recommendations: in one Konya study, 34% of farmers who tested and ignored the results said they did not trust them. Where farmers are told about soil testing, they value it more. I found little direct evidence on the role of dealers or extension in Central Anatolia specifically.

### Cited Findings
- Among farmers who tested but did not follow results, 34% did not trust the analysis results (study cited in Tanrıverdi & Çelik 2016) [seen-page]. [Tanrıverdi & Çelik 2016](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/476123)
- 45.24% of Çumra farm managers felt analysis-based fertilization was not effective enough on yield [seen-page]. [Tanrıverdi & Çelik 2016](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/476123)
- İzmir: 67.80% of farmers said they had been told about the importance of soil analysis. The figure was 83.33% among users of the support and 59.21% among non-users, and the link between being informed and using the support was significant [seen-page]. [Çönoğlu et al. 2016](https://zfdergi.ege.edu.tr/edergiziraat/2016_cilt53/s4/10.pdf)
- Sivas-Hafik: producers set the fertilizer amount, type and timing from their own knowledge and experience. Their information sources are family, neighbor farmers and relatives, which the authors say hinders new technologies [snippet; exact paper URL not identified].
- Ankara-Gölbaşı: 17.64% found the analysis-recommended amount insufficient compared with their experience [snippet]. [silo.tips PDF](https://silo.tips/download/buday-retmnde-toprak-analz-yaptirmanin-letme-zerne-etkler-ankara-glbai-les-rne-s)
- Konya-Cihanbeyli 2023: the mean farmer age was 57.98 and mean wheat experience 24.58 years [seen-page]. [Tapkı & Göz 2025](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/4639595)
- Kayseri and Yozgat: a sugar factory's (Kayseri Şeker) soil analyses and recommendations for beet growers were reported as well received [snippet, news]. [Mynet](https://www.mynet.com/kayseri-sekerin-toprak-analizleri-ciftcinin-yuzunu-guldurdu-180102501120)

### Inferences
- Farmers are older and rely on long experience and peer advice, and recommendations are distrusted when they suggest less than habit. A decision-support site should show (a) its reasons, (b) how its suggestion compares with typical local practice (for example "typical: about 20 kg/da DAP + about 11 kg/da urea"), and (c) the measurement source. That matches the project's badge and reason requirements.
- Integrator-led advice such as the sugar factory example appears to be trusted. This suggests that trusted intermediaries matter more than the information alone.

### Gaps
- I found no 2018–2026 Central Anatolia study that measures the share of farmers relying on fertilizer dealers (bayi), cooperatives (PANKOBİRLİK, Konya Şeker) or public extension (TARGEL, ÇATAK) for fertilizer decisions.
- I found no study on whether farmers value soil-health information such as organic matter or salinity, as distinct from fertilizer dose advice.
- The TRA1 (80.9% experience) and Sivas-Hafik findings come from search summaries only. Their papers were not identified or read.
